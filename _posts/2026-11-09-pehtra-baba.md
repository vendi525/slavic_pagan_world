Between Christmas and Epiphany the nights are the longest and the darkest of the year. These are the twelve nights, and they belong to Pehtra Baba.

## The name

Pehtra means the shining one. Her name comes from an old Germanic word, *perachtum*, "meaning that which glitters" (Kropej).[1] She was one of the *sredozimke*, the midwinter women, who "bring brightness, light and fertility" while they "roam around in the period between Christmas and Epiphany" (Kropej).[1]

## The woman with the golden bucket

The oldest description of her is only a sentence long, written down in 1858, and it's enough to see her:

> "Pehtra Baba roams around on the Karavanke Mountains, strolls around the mountain tops above the Kamnik Alps with a golden bucket in her hand."
>
> <cite>Kropej[1]</cite>

Picture a clear frozen night. Snow on the peaks, stars above them, and a tall old woman walking along the ridge with something gold swinging in her hand. Down in the valley, every house has its fire lit, and every spinner has put her wheel away. Because Pehtra is coming to check.

## The spinners' judge

Pehtra was the keeper of women's work, and she had rules. On certain nights no one was allowed to spin. Women who kept spinning after midnight, or left yarn on the wheel overnight, were punished. One of these midwinter women, the *torka*, "would enter the house and turn off the light. Spinning wheels started to spin by themselves, and did not stop until morning" (Kropej).[2] By dawn the yarn was bitten to pieces and the thread was full of knots.

But she was not only cruel. People left her offerings, "a flock of wool, sheaves of flax or napkins" (Kropej),[2] and a house that kept her rules got her light and fertility for the year ahead.

## The wild hunt

Pehtra did not walk alone. She was "the leader of the wild hunt", which people imagined as "a night-time procession of rushing and raging demons and departed souls during twelve nights around Christmas and New Year" (Kropej).[3] The dead came back in the storm, with dogs and horses, howling through the air.

The wisest thing was to stay inside. One man from Bovec didn't believe in the Perte, so on Epiphany he hid by the bridge to watch them pass:

> "As they were passing one of them hacked at his leg with a broad axe, crippling him. A year later, he waited for them again. The same Pêrta said: "I've forgotten something here last year; I have to take it back." And he was well again."
>
> <cite>Kropej[3]</cite>

That is how the hunt worked: whatever it took, it came back for a year later, at the same place and the same hour. The twelve nights are a door between one year and the next, and between the living and the dead. In this the hunt belongs to two old figures at once, "Pehtra Baba, the primal mother and the leader of souls," and "the god of the underworld Veles" (Kropej).[4]

## How she survived

When the church came, Pehtra's light went to the saints. She "was replaced by St. Lucia, who brings light, and St. Gertrude (Jedrt) who spins flax and yarn" (Kropej).[3] Her darker side went to the witches. But in the Alps she never fully left. Masked Perchten still run through the villages in early January in Austria and Bavaria, ringing bells, some beautiful and some terrifying, and in Slovenian mountain villages Pehta lives on in children's stories.

## What is old and what is new

Old: the midwinter woman who checks on spinners, the ban on spinning on holy nights, and the wild hunt of the dead in the twelve nights. These are shared by Slavic, Germanic and Alpine peoples, and no one can say who had them first.

Newer: her name is Germanic, not Slavic, and the link to Mokosh is a scholar's comparison, not a written myth. The Perchten processions as tourists see them today were shaped in the last two centuries.

## Questions about Pehtra Baba

### Who is Pehtra Baba?

Pehtra Baba is a midwinter woman from Slovenian and Alpine folklore. She walks the mountains during the twelve nights, rewards hard-working spinners, punishes lazy ones and leads the wild hunt.

### What are the twelve nights?

The twelve nights run from Christmas to Epiphany (25 December to 6 January). In folk belief the dead and spirits were close during these nights, and people avoided spinning, washing and travelling.

### Is Pehtra Baba the same as Frau Perchta?

Yes. Pehtra is the Slovene name of the Alpine Perchta, known in Austria and southern Germany. She is related to Frau Holle and is often compared with Baba Yaga.

### What is the wild hunt?

The wild hunt is a stormy night procession of ghosts, dogs and horses that rushes through the sky in the twelve nights. Anyone who meets it should throw themselves to the ground or stay indoors.
