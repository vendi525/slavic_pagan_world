## The name

His name is the forest. "The name Léshy or Lyeshy is derived from lyes, a forest" (Ralston).[1] He is simply "the one of the forest", the same way the Domovoi is "the one of the house".

## The master of the woods

The Leshy can change his size at will, "at one time making himself taller than the trees of the forest, and at another shorter than the grass of the field" (Ralston).[2] When he walks among people he looks like an ordinary peasant in a sheepskin coat, except that it is not belted, and the left side is wrapped over the right. And "he never has any eyebrows or eyelashes" (Ralston).[2]

Every animal in the wood belongs to him. His favourite is the bear, "his only servant, who watches over him when he has taken too much of the strong drink he loves so well" (Ralston).[3] When he goes around his lands, "the forest roars around him and the trees shake" (Ralston).[3]

The Leshies even gamble. In 1843, huge flocks of squirrels crossed parts of Russia, and the peasants knew why: "a Lyeshy in the Vyatka Government had gambled away all his squirrels to a brother demon in that of Vologda, and the lost property was on its way to its new master" (Ralston).[4]

## Lost in the forest

The Leshy's favourite game is getting people lost. He moves the landmarks, takes the shape of a tree you steer by, or walks beside you like a friendly stranger. "His victim would chat away unconcernedly, till, all of a sudden, he found himself in a swamp or ravine. Then a loud laugh would be heard" (Ralston).[5]

Picture the old forest before roads: dark spruce, no sky, every trail looking the same. Getting lost wasn't a story. It happened, and people died of it. So they knew the cure. If you were led astray, you took off your shoes and turned their linings the other way, and turned your coat inside out too (Ralston).[5] The Leshy does everything backwards, so you do too, and his spell breaks.

## How he was kept happy

Hunters and herders made deals with him. "In order to please that wayward spirit," a hunter "makes an offering of a piece of bread, or a pancake, sprinkled with salt, and lays it on the stump of a tree" (Ralston).[4] Some left him the first animal they caught. In the north, herders who pleased the Leshy believed he would pasture the village cattle for them, while those who forgot him could lose the whole herd (Ralston).[2]

And when someone came home from the woods sick, people said "He has crossed the Lyeshy's track." The sick man wrapped bread and salt in a clean cloth, carried it back to the forest, and left it there with a prayer, along with his illness (Ralston).[5]

## What is old and what is new

Old: the Leshy, his tricks and the offerings to him are recorded across Russia, Ukraine and Belarus in the 1800s, and similar forest beings are known to all Slavs.

Newer: his horns, goat feet and claws came with Christianity, when every spirit began to look like the devil. Ralston notes this himself. The link between the Leshy and Veles is a modern idea.

## Questions about the Leshy

### What is a Leshy?

A Leshy is the Slavic spirit of the forest. He protects the trees and animals, can change his size and shape, and likes to lead travellers astray.

### What does a Leshy look like?

Usually like a peasant in a sheepskin coat with no belt, no eyebrows and no eyelashes. He can grow as tall as the trees or shrink as small as the grass.

### What do you do if a Leshy gets you lost?

Take off your shoes, turn their linings the other way, and turn your coat inside out. Then the Leshy's spell is broken and you can find your way.

### Is the Leshy evil?

No. He is wild and tricky, but he helps hunters and herders who respect him and leave him bread and salt.
