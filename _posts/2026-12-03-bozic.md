## The name

Božič simply means "little god." As early as 1848, writers noted that the word "božič denotes a young or small god" (Kropej).[1] He is the son of Svarog, the old god of the sky, whose name comes from "svaro- meaning the sky firmament" (Kropej).[1] Svarožič, little Svarog, the son of heaven.

Like the Greek Dionysus, "Svarog was allegedly born, each year anew, on the winter solstice" (Kropej).[1] When Christianity came, the people kept the name and gave it to a new divine child. That is why, to this day, Christmas in Slovene is Božič.

## The birth of the young sun

The old year dies slowly. The days shrink, the nights grow long and cold, and the sun hangs low and tired over the fields. Then, at the solstice, something is born.

"People believed that during the period around the winter solstice, when the days are the shortest and nights the longest, the young sun is born" (Kropej).[2]

But the sun does not come easily. In Slavic myth, it is locked in the dark until the thunder god fights for it. "Once Perun defeats Veles, Božič is released, or born" (Kropej).[2] The sky god strikes the dragon of the underworld, and out of the darkness the little light breaks free.

## The Christmas foal

In folk songs the young sun is not a baby but a horse. "In folk songs, Božič is referred to as the Christmas foal" (Kropej).[2]

Carolers sang of a black horse tied to a green pine, with the master's little son sitting in the saddle. In some villages children fasted all day on Christmas Eve, because those who ate nothing would see the foal in the evening. Families left a basket of hay out for it, so the foal would come in the night "to eat hay under the table" (Kropej).[4]

A young horse, wild and strong, running across the sky. It is how the old year imagined the new one: not as a gentle light, but as something alive that needs to be fed.

## The god in the fire

On Christmas Eve, the great log burns on the hearth. Among the South Slavs it is called the {% include wiki-link.html slug="koleda" text="badnjak" %}, and the young god himself was sometimes called by the same name.[1] On that night, families sat by the fire and watched the shadows. In one village, people looked at the reflections cast by the burning log, "saying that Božič jumps around the fireplace" (Kropej).[3]

Every spark that leaps out of the fire is the newborn sun, dancing.

## Questions about Božič

### Who is Božič in Slavic mythology?

Božič, also called Svarožič, is the young Slavic sun god, the son of Svarog, born each year at the winter solstice. His name means "little god."

### Why is Christmas called Božič?

When Christianity arrived, the name of the young sun god born at the solstice passed to the newborn Christ. That is why Christmas is Božič in Slovene.

### What is the myth of Božič?

The young sun is trapped in darkness until Perun, the thunder god, defeats Veles, the god of the underworld. Then Božič is released and born.

### What is the Christmas foal?

In folk songs and customs, Božič appears as a young horse. Families left hay for the Christmas foal on Christmas Eve.
