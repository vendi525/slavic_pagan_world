At the end of winter, when the snow turns grey and the ice on the river starts to crack, the village makes a doll of straw and rags. They dress her in white. Then they carry her out of the village and throw her into the water. Her name is Morana, and she is winter, and she is death.

## The name

Her name comes from the old Slavic root *mor-*, the same root as in words for death, plague and nightmare. When Ralston described the tree used in a Russian midsummer rite, he gave its name and translated it in brackets: "Marena [Winter or Death]" (Ralston).[4] Winter and death were one and the same word.

## Carrying out Death

All over the Slavic world, people did the same thing as winter ended. "In every Slavonic country, indeed, there are to be found, at this period of the year, traces of olden rites intended to typify the death of Winter and the birth of Spring," above all "the destruction of a figure representing Winter or Death" (Ralston).[1]

In Poland the doll was made of hemp and straw and "flung into a pond or swamp, with the words "The Devil take thee!"" Then everyone ran home, and whoever fell on the way would die within the year (Ralston).[1]

In Lusatia the figure of Death was tied to a long pole and pelted with sticks and stones. "Whoever knocks it off the pole is certain to live through the year." Then the bearers threw it into the water or across the village boundary, and came home "carrying green boughs or an entire tree, emblems of the springtide life which has taken the place of banished death" (Ralston).[2]

Picture it: a grey March morning, a crowd of girls singing, a white straw figure bobbing on a pole above their heads. At the river they stop, and the singing gets louder. Splash. Then they turn back with their arms full of green branches, and the village feels lighter.

## The woman with the sickle

Morana was not just a doll. Sometimes she was "dressed in white, as if in a shroud," with a broom in one hand "in token of winter's sweeping storms," and in the other a sickle, the sign of "the goddess whom the Old Slavonians represented as reaping the living harvest of the world" (Ralston).[2]

Slovenes saw Death the same way: "an old fearsome woman clad in white or as a tall, bony white woman with a scythe in her hand" (Kropej).[5]

In Poland, where she is Marzanna, the rite was believed "to protect people against sudden, untimely death" (Dixon-Kennedy).[3] You couldn't drown death itself. But you could send away the death that comes too early, and keep the village safe for another year.

## Morana at midsummer

Morana comes back once more in the year. At Kupala, the midsummer feast, "in many parts of the country figures of Kupalo and Marena are made and afterwards drowned and burned" (Máchal).[6] Winter is drowned in spring, and in summer the light itself is drowned, as the days begin to shorten again. The wheel turns.

## How she survived

Drowning Morana is still done today. In Czechia, Slovakia, Poland and Slovenia, school classes and villages make a Morana doll on the fifth Sunday of Lent or on the first day of spring, carry it to the river and throw it in, singing that they are carrying death out and bringing summer in.

## What is old and what is new

Old: the rite itself, the straw figure of Winter or Death drowned or burned in spring. The church tried to stop the "Driving out of Death" as early as the 14th century, and failed (Máchal).[7]

New: Morana as a great goddess of winter and death, with her own myths, a husband and a palace, comes from modern writers. In the old sources she is a name, a doll and a rite, and that is already a lot.

## Questions about Morana

### Who is Morana in Slavic mythology?

Morana (Marzanna, Marena) is the Slavic figure of winter and death. She is best known from the spring rite of drowning or burning her straw effigy.

### When is Morana drowned?

Traditionally on the fifth Sunday of Lent, called Death Sunday, or around the spring equinox on 21 March. In some places it was done at the end of Shrovetide.

### Why do people drown Morana?

To drive winter and death out of the village and welcome spring. Throwing her into water or fire sends her away for another year.

### Is Morana a goddess?

She may have been once, but no old source describes her worship. What survives is the rite of carrying out Death, and the name Morana for the straw figure.
