And here's this week's story to sit with.

Death in Tehran

A rich man in Persia was walking in his garden with his servant. Suddenly the servant went pale. He had just seen Death standing among the trees, and Death had looked straight at him.

He begged his master for the fastest horse. If he rode hard, he could reach Tehran by nightfall, far away from here.

The master gave him the horse, and the servant was gone in a cloud of dust.

Later that day the master walked back into the garden, and there was Death. "Why did you frighten my servant?" he asked.

"I didn't mean to," said Death. "I was only surprised to see him here. I'm meeting him tonight, in Tehran."

An old Persian tale, retold. Viktor Frankl tells it in Man's Search for Meaning.
